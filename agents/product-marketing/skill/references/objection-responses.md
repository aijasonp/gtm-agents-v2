# Objection responses

One prepared response per objection category. The agent matches the `## category` heading
to the objection key it finds in call evidence. Edit these to your own words; keep each one
short: acknowledge, ask, answer, next step. Never promise a feature or a price here that
`company.yaml` does not back.

## price

Acknowledge the number is real. Ask what they are comparing it to: another tool, a hire, or
doing nothing. Tie the cost to the outcome they named on the call (hours saved, ramp time,
forecast accuracy). Offer a scoped pilot with clear success criteria instead of a discount.

## timing

Ask what has to happen first and when. If a real event sits ahead (budget cycle, reorg,
hiring wave), agree a date to revisit and what you will bring. If nothing is blocking, ask what
waiting costs them per month using their own numbers.

## authority

Ask who else weighs in and what each person cares about. Offer a short summary they can
forward, written in the economic buyer's terms. Ask to join the next internal conversation
for ten minutes to answer questions directly.

## competition

Ask what they like about the current option and what made them take this call anyway. Do not
attack the other vendor. Point to the one or two places where the evidence says we differ, and
offer a side by side on their own data.

## need

Ask how they handle the problem today and what it costs. If the problem is truly small for them,
say so and step back. If it is real but not felt, share what teams their size usually see, only
from approved proof points.

## security

Ask for their review checklist early. Share the standard security packet. Offer a call with
the person who owns security answers. Put the review on the mutual plan with a date.

## integration

Ask which systems must connect and who owns them. Confirm what works today and what does not;
never promise a roadmap item. Offer a technical session with their admin.
