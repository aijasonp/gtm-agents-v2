# Objection playbook

Pattern for every objection: acknowledge it in one line, ask one question, then answer only what they told you. Never argue, never list features.

## price
- Acknowledge: "Fair. It is a real line item."
- Ask: "What are you comparing it to: another tool, a headcount, or doing nothing?"
- Then: tie cost to the metric they gave you. If they gave none, go back to metrics discovery.

## timing
- Acknowledge: "Makes sense if other things are on fire."
- Ask: "What happens to the problem if this waits a quarter?"
- Then: offer a smaller first step that fits this quarter, or agree a dated check-in.

## authority
- Acknowledge: "Of course, they should weigh in."
- Ask: "What will they ask you that I can help answer?"
- Then: offer a short summary they can forward, and ask for 20 minutes with them.

## competition
- Acknowledge: "Good to know what you have today."
- Ask: "What works well with it, and what made you take this call anyway?"
- Then: compare on the gap they named, using the competitor positioning in company.yaml. No attacks.

## need
- Acknowledge: "Then it may not be a fit, and that is fine."
- Ask: "How do you handle it today, and how long does it take?"
- Then: if the answer shows no pain, disqualify politely.

## security
- Acknowledge: "Sensible to check early."
- Ask: "Who runs the review, and what do they need first?"
- Then: send the security pack and start the review in parallel with the evaluation.

## integration
- Acknowledge: "It has to fit what you run."
- Ask: "Which systems does it need to talk to on day one?"
- Then: confirm the specific connector or API, or say plainly it is not supported.
